# Synopsis

## AI Governance At Scale

---

Most enterprise AI governance efforts do not fail because the organisation lacked the right principles. They fail because there was no operating system to make those principles real. A responsible AI statement, a governance committee, and a signed set of commitments are not governance. They are the beginning of an operating model problem that most organisations have not yet recognised as one.

**AI Governance At Scale** is the field guide for closing that gap.

It gives governance, risk and technology leaders a complete way to reason about enterprise AI governance, built on a single argument and independent of any cloud, model or vendor: AI governance is an operating model problem. The principles are one component within a larger system, and the quality of that system, not the elegance of the principles, determines whether AI is used safely, lawfully, fairly and accountably at scale. No hype. No ethics washing. Every mechanism is examined as a governance review, with its trade-offs and failure modes made explicit and a clear verdict on when to reach for it and when to leave it alone.

The book moves through six parts: **Thinking About AI Governance** (the bridge from IT, data, risk and audit disciplines, and the enterprise problem space that makes AI governance distinct), **The Foundations of Control** (risk classification, accountability and the operating model, and the design of policy, standards and controls), **Governing the AI Lifecycle** (data governance, evaluation and validation, deployment and change control, monitoring and incident response, and decommissioning), **Responsible AI in Practice** (fairness, transparency and contestability, privacy, safety and security, and meaningful human oversight, turned from principles into enforceable controls), **Regulation, Assurance and Sector Governance** (cross cutting regulation, assurance and evidence, and the sector regimes that layer on top), and **Scaling and Sustaining Governance** (generative and agentic AI, operating models and maturity, and the economics and proportionality that keep governance sustainable).

Written for every leader who has watched the organisation publish its AI principles, then faced the harder question on Monday morning: "The statement promises fairness, oversight and accountability. Now how do I make those real, and provable, across many teams, vendors, models and jurisdictions at once?"

The principles are a component. The operating system is the product.
